1. Objective

Establish whether body-local CER increases detected by ASR (faster-whisper-small) correspond to human-perceivable audio degradation in 7 catastrophe events from a prospective N=200 TTS study.

Core question: When ASR detects body text damage after an opener is prepended, can human listeners also hear the damage?

3. Experimental Protocol (G2-G5)

G2: Human Blind Listening

  • Annotators: 3 Japanese-native listeners (required; cannot be automated)
  • Protocol: Randomized order playback, no reference text shown, no ASR output shown
  • Task: Dictate exactly what you hear. Write every character. If you cannot hear clearly, write what you guess.
  • Conditions per event: T0 (baseline) and T1l (long opener) for each backend
  • Blinding: Annotators do not know which condition they are hearing

G3: Body Text Extraction

  • Using the pre-defined body boundary (same prefix proxy as ASR analysis), separate each human transcription into opener and body portions
  • Record boundary location and any ambiguity notes

G4: Independent CER Computation

  • Compute human body CER using identical hiragana normalization as ASR pipeline
  • Formula: L_i^H(a) = CER(H_i(a), c_i) where H_i(a) is annotator's transcription and c_i is reference body text
  • Compute per-annotator, per-audio, per-condition

G5: Statistical Comparison

  • Primary metric: Human body ST = L_i^H(T1l) - L_i^H(T0)
  • Comparison: Human ST vs ASR ST per event (direction agreement, not magnitude match)
  • Inter-annotator agreement: Fleiss' kappa for categorical judgments; ICC for continuous CER

ASR detects damage, human does notBody CER increase is ASR-specific (recognizer sensitivity to opener conditions)Supports ASR-mediated audit; cannot claim perceptual degradation
Human detects damage, ASR does notDetector has false negatives; FN rate is the validity threatRequires reporting ASR coverage limitations

Note: These 7 events are selected for extreme ASR-detected damage. Results cannot be generalized to estimate population-level catastrophe rates without expansion to the full N=200 content pool.

5. Limitations

  • 7 events are ASR-selected extremes, not a random sample
  • Results are case evidence, not population estimates
  • Hiragana normalization may not capture all perceptual differences (e.g., kanji substitution)
  • 3 annotators provide inter-rater reliability but not population-level human performance estimates
  • Original N=60 paper's catastrophe events are different from these N=200 events (different data, different backends)
